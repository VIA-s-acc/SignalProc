## Block parameters

### `wav` — Audio file

- `path` — File path; a relative path is resolved against the pipeline's folder

### `samples` — Samples

- `values` — Sample values separated by spaces, commas or semicolons; channels are separated by a bar |, e.g. 1 2 3 | 4 5 6
- `rate` — Sample rate, Hz: how many samples make one second

### `add` — Adder ⊕

- `op` — Operation on the inputs: a + b or a − b
  - `add` — sum a + b
  - `sub` — difference a − b

### `gen` — Generator

- `kind` — Which signal to make; it decides which of the other parameters apply
  - `sine` — sine at freq with peak amplitude
  - `sweep` — logarithmic sweep from freq to f1 at amplitude 1
  - `impulse` — unit impulse at the first sample, zeros after it
  - `step` — unit step: ones from the first sample on
  - `white` — white Gaussian noise; amplitude is its RMS, seed picks the draw
  - `pink` — pink 1/f noise; amplitude is its RMS, seed picks the draw
  - `harmonics` — tone at freq plus overtones 2f, 3f, 4f at a, a/2, a/4, a/8
  - `doppler` — Donoho–Johnstone Doppler: an oscillation whose period grows
  - `blocks` — Donoho–Johnstone Blocks: piecewise constant with jumps
  - `bumps` — Donoho–Johnstone Bumps: sharp peaks on a zero baseline
  - `heavisine` — Donoho–Johnstone HeaviSine: a sine with two jumps
- `freq` — Frequency, Hz: of the tone and the harmonics' fundamental, start of the sweep
- `f1` — End frequency of the sweep, Hz; the other kinds ignore it
- `amplitude` — Amplitude (RMS for noise); sweep, impulse and step ignore it
- `rate` — Sample rate, Hz; frequencies above half of it alias
- `duration` — Signal length, s
- `seed` — Noise generator seed: the same seed gives the same noise; white and pink only
- `channels` — Number of channels; noise gets its own realisation in each channel (seed, seed + 1, …), other kinds repeat in every channel

### `gain` — Gain

- `db` — Gain, dB: +6 roughly doubles the amplitude, negative values attenuate

### `normalize` — Normalize

- `kind` — Which level to match: the peak or the RMS
  - `peak` — max |x| is set to target_dbfs
  - `rms` — the RMS value is set to target_dbfs
- `target_dbfs` — Target level in dB relative to full scale (0 dBFS is amplitude 1.0)

### `clip` — Clip

- `limit` — Clipping level: anything beyond ±limit is cut to it

### `quantize` — Quantize

- `bits` — Bit depth: fewer bits mean a coarser step and louder quantisation noise
- `dither` — Whether to add triangular (TPDF) noise before rounding
  - `yes` — TPDF dither: the error becomes steady noise unrelated to the signal
  - `no` — plain rounding: the error follows the signal and sounds like distortion
- `seed` — Dither generator seed; channel c uses seed + c

### `mulaw` — μ-law

- `direction` — Direction: compress (encode) or expand (decode)
  - `encode` — compress: y = sign(x)·ln(1 + μ|x|)/ln(1 + μ), input clipped to ±1
  - `decode` — expand: the inverse map, restoring the original scale
- `mu` — The μ parameter: larger values squeeze loud samples harder relative to quiet ones

### `split` — Channels

- `mode` — What goes to outputs l and r
  - `lr` — l gets channel 0, r channel 1 (mono feeds channel 0 to both)
  - `channel` — both outputs get the channel numbered channel
- `channel` — Zero-based channel number for mode channel

### `mid_side` — M/S

- `direction` — Direction; the rotation is orthogonal, hence lossless
  - `encode` — L/R → M/S: channel 0 becomes mid, channel 1 side
  - `decode` — M/S → L/R: recovers left and right

### `delay` — Delay

- `samples` — Delay in samples, or seconds with an s suffix (0.01s)

### `cut` — Cut

- `begin` — Start of the part, inclusive: samples, or seconds with an s suffix (0.01s)
- `end` — End of the piece, excluded; 0 means to the end of the signal. Samples or seconds with s (0.01s)

### `downsample` — Downsample ↓M

- `m` — Downsampling factor M: the sample rate is divided by M
- `attenuation_db` — Alias suppression of the Kaiser filter, dB: more is cleaner but the filter is longer
- `anti_alias` — Whether to low-pass at fs/(2M) before dropping samples
  - `yes` — Kaiser low-pass first, then every M-th sample: no aliasing
  - `no` — just every M-th sample: content above fs/(2M) folds onto low frequencies
- `phase` — Downsampling offset: p keeps x[p], x[p+M], …; must be below M

### `upsample` — Upsample ↑M

- `m` — Upsampling factor M: the sample rate is multiplied by M
- `attenuation_db` — Image suppression of the Kaiser filter, dB: more is cleaner but the filter is longer
- `anti_image` — Whether to low-pass the zero-stuffed signal to remove spectral images
  - `yes` — Kaiser low-pass with gain M: a smooth interpolation of the input
  - `no` — zero stuffing only: the spectrum keeps M−1 images

### `resample` — Resample L/M

- `l` — Upsampling factor L, the numerator of the rate ratio
- `m` — Downsampling factor M, the denominator: the new rate is fs·L/M
- `attenuation_db` — Image and alias suppression of the Kaiser filters, dB: more is cleaner but longer

### `convolve` — Convolution

- `filter` — Filter as wavelet.role: h0/h1 analysis low/high-pass, f0/f1 synthesis; or a library name
- `method` — How to compute it; results match up to rounding
  - `auto` — FFT when both operands are at least 160 samples long, else direct
  - `direct` — direct sum of products, O(N·L): faster for short filters
  - `fft` — via FFT, O((N+L) log(N+L)): faster for long filters

### `dwt` — DWT

- `wavelet` — Wavelet name (haar, db4, sym8, coif3, bior2.2, dmey…): sets the filter bank
- `levels` — Number of levels J; 0 picks the maximum for this length minus 3, at least 1
- `ext` — How the signal is extended beyond its edges; this sets the band lengths
  - `symmetric` — mirror repeating the edge: … x1 x0 | x0 x1 …
  - `periodization` — circular, non-expansive: ⌈N/2⌉ coefficients per level
  - `zero` — zeros beyond the edges
  - `constant` — repeats the edge sample
  - `smooth` — linear extrapolation from the two edge samples
  - `periodic` — periodic repetition, bands get longer
  - `reflect` — mirror without repeating the edge: … x2 x1 | x0 x1 x2 …
  - `antisymmetric` — mirror with a sign flip: … −x1 −x0 | x0 x1 …
  - `antireflect` — point reflection about the edge sample: x[−n] = 2x0 − x[n]

### `idwt` — IDWT

- `wavelet` — Synthesis wavelet: auto reuses the analysis one; any other name must match it
- `ext` — Edge extension: auto reuses the analysis mode; any other value must match it
  - `auto` — use the mode stored with the coefficients
  - `symmetric` — mirror repeating the edge: … x1 x0 | x0 x1 …
  - `periodization` — circular, non-expansive: ⌈N/2⌉ coefficients per level
  - `zero` — zeros beyond the edges
  - `constant` — repeats the edge sample
  - `smooth` — linear extrapolation from the two edge samples
  - `periodic` — periodic repetition, bands get longer
  - `reflect` — mirror without repeating the edge: … x2 x1 | x0 x1 x2 …
  - `antisymmetric` — mirror with a sign flip: … −x1 −x0 | x0 x1 …
  - `antireflect` — point reflection about the edge sample: x[−n] = 2x0 − x[n]

### `swt` — SWT

- `wavelet` — Wavelet name (haar, db4, sym8, coif3, bior2.2, dmey…): sets the filter bank
- `levels` — Number of levels J; 0 picks it; the signal is periodically padded to a multiple of 2^J
- `norm` — Whether to divide the filters by √2 so band energy equals signal energy
  - `no` — DWT filters as is (as in pywt): energy doubles at every level
  - `yes` — filters ×1/√2: the total band energy equals the signal energy

### `lift53` — Lifting 5/3

- `levels` — Number of levels; the signal needs at least 2^levels samples
- `integer` — Whether to compute in integers, the reversible lossless JPEG 2000 variant
  - `no` — floating-point lifting: perfect reconstruction up to rounding
  - `yes` — samples ×32768 to integers, integer lifting: 16-bit PCM comes back exactly

### `iswt` — ISWT

- `wavelet` — Synthesis wavelet: auto reuses the SWT one; any other name must match it

### `threshold` — Threshold

- `rule` — How the threshold λ is chosen per level; the approximation is left alone
  - `bayes` — BayesShrink: λ = σ²/σx per level, adapts to the signal
  - `visu` — VisuShrink: λ = σ√(2 ln N), removes nearly all noise but oversmooths
  - `sure` — SureShrink: λ minimises Stein's unbiased risk estimate per level
  - `minimax` — Donoho–Johnstone minimax threshold: milder than the universal one
- `mode` — Shrinkage function: how coefficients change relative to λ
  - `soft` — soft: |y| ≤ λ → 0, the rest shrink by λ; smooth but biased
  - `hard` — hard: |y| ≤ λ → 0, the rest kept; less bias, more residual noise
  - `garrote` — non-negative garrote: y − λ²/y when |y| > λ, between soft and hard
- `levels` — Which detail levels to process: J or J1..J2; 1 is the finest, d1
- `sigma` — Noise level σ: mad_d1 estimates it as median|d1|/0.6745, or give a number

### `band_edit` — Band edit

- `enabled` — Bands to keep, comma-separated (a, d1, d3) or all; the others are zeroed
- `gains_db` — Band gains in dB, comma-separated, like d2=-6, a=3; other bands stay as is

### `xcorr` — Correlation

- `mode` — Which part of the correlation to output, as in numpy.correlate
  - `full` — all lags: length Na + Nb − 1
  - `same` — the central part, as long as input a
  - `valid` — only fully overlapping lags: length |Na − Nb| + 1
- `normalize` — Whether to divide by √(Ea·Eb) so values lie in [−1, 1]
  - `no` — raw sums of products: the scale depends on the signal levels
  - `yes` — correlation coefficient: 1 means the same shape at that lag

### `denoise` — Denoise

- `wavelet` — Wavelet for the decomposition; long smooth ones (sym8, db8) suit smooth signals
- `levels` — Number of levels J; 0 picks the maximum for this length minus 3, at least 1
- `rule` — How the threshold λ is chosen per level; σ is estimated from the MAD of d1
  - `bayes` — BayesShrink: λ = σ²/σx per level, adapts to the signal
  - `visu` — VisuShrink: λ = σ√(2 ln N), removes nearly all noise but oversmooths
  - `sure` — SureShrink: λ minimises Stein's unbiased risk estimate per level
  - `minimax` — Donoho–Johnstone minimax threshold: milder than the universal one
- `mode` — Shrinkage function: how coefficients change relative to λ
  - `soft` — soft: |y| ≤ λ → 0, the rest shrink by λ; smooth but biased
  - `hard` — hard: |y| ≤ λ → 0, the rest kept; less bias, more residual noise
- `shifts` — Circular shifts to average over (cycle spinning): more means fewer artefacts, slower

### `thd` — THD

- `harmonics` — Highest harmonic counted: the 2nd to Nth below Nyquist enter the THD

### `onsets` — Onsets

- `n` — FFT window length, samples: longer is finer in frequency, coarser in time
- `hop` — Window hop, samples: smaller gives finer onset times but takes longer

### `export` — Export CSV / JSON

- `path` — File path; a relative path is resolved against the pipeline's folder
- `format` — File format
  - `csv` — table: column n plus one column ch0, ch1… per channel
  - `json` — object {rate, channels: [[…], …]}: the rate and channel arrays

### `export_report` — Export report

- `path` — File path; a relative path is resolved against the pipeline's folder
- `format` — File format
  - `csv` — two columns field,value, one row per field
  - `json` — object with passed and fields (name → value)

### `check` — Check

- `kind` — Pass criterion; a channel count mismatch or a short input always fails
  - `pr` — perfect reconstruction: max|x̂ − x| at most tol
  - `error` — error budget: max|x̂ − x| at most tol, for approximate schemes
  - `snr` — reconstruction SNR in dB at least tol
- `tol` — Threshold: the largest error for pr and error, the smallest SNR in dB for snr
- `delay` — How far the tested signal lags the reference, in samples, or auto to find it

### `wav_out` — Write

- `path` — File path; a relative path is resolved against the pipeline's folder
- `bits` — Sample format in the file
  - `16` — 16-bit integer PCM, CD quality
  - `24` — 24-bit integer PCM, the studio standard
  - `32` — 32-bit integer PCM
  - `f32` — 32-bit float: values beyond full scale are kept, not clipped
- `dither` — Dither when rounding to an integer format; ignored for f32
  - `none` — plain rounding: the error follows the signal
  - `tpdf` — triangular ±1 LSB noise before rounding: the error becomes steady noise
- `seed` — Dither generator seed: the same seed writes the same file

### `pad` — Zero padding

- `before` — Zeros to insert at the start: samples, or seconds with an s suffix (0.01s)
- `after` — Zeros to append at the end: samples, or seconds with an s suffix (0.01s)

### `multiply` — Multiplier ⊗

- `factor` — Constant factor; a negative value flips the polarity

### `window` — Window

- `kind` — Window shape: a trade-off between main-lobe width and side-lobe level
  - `hann` — Hann: side lobes at −31 dB, a good general choice
  - `hamming` — Hamming: first side lobe at −43 dB, but slow roll-off
  - `blackman` — Blackman: side lobes at −58 dB, a wider main lobe
  - `kaiser` — Kaiser: the shape is set by beta
  - `tukey` — Tukey: flat middle with cosine tapers, alpha is their share
- `beta` — Kaiser parameter: higher lowers the side lobes and widens the main lobe; kaiser only
- `alpha` — Share of the length in the Tukey tapers: 0 is rectangular, 1 is Hann; tukey only

### `fade` — Fade in and out

- `fade_in` — Fade-in length in samples, or seconds with an s suffix (0.01s); 0 turns it off
- `fade_out` — Fade-out length in samples, or seconds with an s suffix (0.01s); 0 turns it off
- `curve` — Shape of the gain curve
  - `linear` — linear ramp from 0 to 1
  - `cosine` — raised half-cosine: a gentle start and end of the ramp

### `softclip` — Soft clip (tanh)

- `drive` — Gain before tanh: higher means stronger saturation and distortion

### `dc_offset` — DC offset

- `value` — Offset in sample units (1.0 is full scale)

### `trim_silence` — Trim silence

- `threshold_db` — Silence threshold, dBFS: edges quieter than it in every channel are cut

### `add_noise` — Add noise

- `color` — Noise colour: the slope of its spectrum
  - `white` — white: equal power at all frequencies
  - `pink` — pink 1/f: −3 dB per octave, equal power in every octave
  - `brown` — brown 1/f²: −6 dB per octave, dominated by lows
- `snr_db` — Signal-to-noise ratio, dB, over the mean power of all channels: lower is noisier
- `seed` — Noise generator seed; channel c uses seed + c

### `chirp` — Chirp

- `method` — How the frequency changes over time
  - `linear` — linear: equal Hz per unit time
  - `log` — logarithmic: equal octaves per unit time; f0 and f1 must be above 0
- `f0` — Start frequency, Hz
- `f1` — End frequency, Hz; below f0 the chirp falls
- `amplitude` — Peak amplitude; a negative value flips the polarity
- `rate` — Sample rate, Hz; frequencies above half of it alias
- `duration` — Signal length, s

### `osc` — Square, sawtooth, triangle

- `kind` — Waveform
  - `square` — rectangular ±1; duty is the share of the period at +1
  - `sawtooth` — sawtooth: rises from −1 to 1 over width of the period, then falls
  - `triangle` — triangle: a sawtooth with width = 0.5
  - `pulses` — a unit pulse at the start of every period, zeros in between
- `freq` — Repetition frequency, Hz
- `duty` — Duty cycle: share of the period at +1; square only
- `width` — Rising share of the sawtooth period: 1 ramps up, 0 ramps down; sawtooth only
- `amplitude` — Amplitude; a negative value flips the polarity
- `rate` — Sample rate, Hz; frequencies above half of it alias
- `duration` — Signal length, s

### `iir` — IIR filter

- `family` — Filter family: shape of the magnitude and phase response
  - `butterworth` — Butterworth: maximally flat passband, no ripple
  - `cheby1` — Chebyshev I: ripple rp in the passband, steeper roll-off
  - `cheby2` — Chebyshev II: flat passband, attenuation rs in the stopband
  - `ellip` — elliptic: ripple rp and rs, the steepest roll-off for the order
  - `bessel` — Bessel: nearly constant group delay, gentle roll-off
- `type` — Band type
  - `lowpass` — low-pass: passes below cutoff
  - `highpass` — high-pass: passes above cutoff
  - `bandpass` — band-pass: passes between the two cutoff frequencies
  - `bandstop` — band-stop: rejects between the two cutoff frequencies
- `order` — Filter order: higher is steeper but rings longer
- `cutoff` — Cutoff frequency, Hz; two, space- or comma-separated, for band-pass and band-stop
- `rp` — Passband ripple, dB; for cheby1 and ellip
- `rs` — Stopband attenuation, dB; for cheby2 and ellip
- `zero_phase` — Whether to filter forward and backward (filtfilt) to cancel the phase shift
  - `no` — ordinary causal filtering: delay and phase distortion
  - `yes` — forward and backward: zero phase, squared magnitude, doubled order

### `fir` — FIR filter (window method)

- `numtaps` — Number of taps N: more gives a sharper transition but a (N−1)/2-sample delay
- `cutoff` — Cutoff frequencies, Hz, space- or comma-separated: one for low/high-pass, more for bands
- `window` — Window used to truncate the ideal response
  - `hamming` — Hamming: about 53 dB stopband, the usual choice
  - `hann` — Hann: about 44 dB stopband
  - `blackman` — Blackman: about 74 dB stopband, wider transition
  - `kaiser` — Kaiser: the attenuation is set by beta
  - `boxcar` — rectangular: narrowest transition, only about 21 dB stopband
- `beta` — Kaiser parameter: higher gives more attenuation and a wider transition; kaiser only
- `pass_zero` — Whether the filter passes zero frequency (DC)
  - `yes` — DC passes: one cutoff gives low-pass, two give band-stop
  - `no` — DC is blocked: one cutoff gives high-pass, two give band-pass
- `compensate` — Whether to remove the filter's (N−1)/2-sample group delay
  - `no` — output lags by (N−1)/2 samples, like a real causal filter
  - `yes` — output shifted back by (N−1)/2, time-aligned with the input

### `median` — Median filter

- `kernel` — Window length, odd, samples: longer removes wider spikes and smooths more

### `moving_average` — Moving average

- `length` — Averaging window, samples: longer smooths more
- `centred` — Whether to centre the window, removing the (length−1)/2 delay
  - `no` — causal window over past samples: output lags by (length−1)/2
  - `yes` — window around the current sample: no lag

### `preemphasis` — Pre-emphasis

- `coeff` — Coefficient k: closer to 1 boosts highs and cuts lows more

### `deemphasis` — De-emphasis

- `coeff` — Coefficient k, the same as in pre-emphasis; closer to 1 means longer memory

### `lift97` — Lifting 9/7

- `levels` — Number of levels; the signal needs at least 2^levels samples

### `dtcwt` — DTCWT

- `levels` — Number of levels J; the band of level j holds complex coefficients with step 2ʲ, stored as re, im pairs
- `biort` — Level 1 filters: biorthogonal and near-symmetric, the two trees take them one sample apart
  - `near_sym_a` — 5 and 7 taps, the default of the dtcwt package
  - `near_sym_b` — 13 and 19 taps: smoother and narrower in frequency
  - `antonini` — 9 and 7 taps, the Antonini filters (CDF 9/7)
  - `legall` — 5 and 3 taps, LeGall 5/3
- `qshift` — Q-shift filters for level 2 and up: the trees have delays of ¼ and ¾ sample, so they drift half a sample apart
  - `qshift_a` — 10 taps, the default of the dtcwt package
  - `qshift_b` — 14 taps
  - `qshift_c` — 16 taps
  - `qshift_d` — 18 taps: the wavelet closest to analytic
  - `qshift_06` — 10 taps of which 6 are non-zero: Kingsbury's early design

### `mra` — MRA component

- `component` — Band to keep: a (or aJ) for the approximation, dJ for the details of level J

### `pan` — Pan

- `position` — Position: −1 is hard left, 0 centre, 1 hard right
- `law` — Pan law: how the level splits between L and R
  - `constant_power` — constant power: L = cos, R = sin; −3 dB each at centre
  - `linear` — linear: L = (1−p)/2, R = (1+p)/2; −6 dB each at centre

### `duplicate` — Duplicate channels

- `channels` — Number of output channels; input channels repeat cyclically

### `assert` — Assert

- `expr` — The expression: report fields as a.max_abs, b.snr_db, a.passed; + − * / ^, comparisons, && || !, abs min max sqrt log10, pi

### `loudness` — Loudness (RMS over 400 ms)

- `window` — Window length in samples, or seconds with an s suffix (0.01s)
- `hop` — Window hop in samples, or seconds with an s suffix (0.01s); smaller is denser

### `lms` — Adaptive LMS/NLMS filter

- `taps` — Filter length, taps: more models a longer path but converges slower
- `mu` — Step size: larger converges faster but noisier; too large makes LMS diverge
- `rule` — Weight update rule
  - `nlms` — normalised LMS: step divided by the energy of x, stable for mu < 2
  - `lms` — classic LMS: unnormalised step that depends on the level of x

### `wiener` — Wiener filter

- `size` — Window for the local mean and variance, samples: larger smooths more
- `noise` — Noise variance; 0 estimates it as the mean local variance

### `spectral_subtract` — Spectral subtraction

- `alpha` — Over-subtraction factor: higher removes more noise but distorts more
- `beta` — Spectral floor: the share of noise power a bin never drops below
- `n` — FFT frame length, samples, hop n/4: longer is finer in frequency, coarser in time
- `noise_s` — Without a noise input, how many seconds at the start count as noise

### `deconvolve` — Deconvolution

- `snr_db` — Expected SNR, dB: higher inverts more boldly but amplifies more noise

### `tremolo` — Tremolo

- `rate_hz` — Level modulation frequency, Hz: 2–10 Hz is a classic tremolo
- `depth` — Depth: 0 leaves the signal unchanged, 1 dips the level to zero
- `shape` — Shape of the low-frequency oscillator (LFO) that sets the level
  - `sine` — sine: a smooth swell
  - `triangle` — triangle: a linear rise and fall
  - `square` — square: hard switching between the full and the reduced level

### `modulated_delay` — Modulated delay

- `mode` — Effect: how the delayed and the original signals combine
  - `chorus` — chorus: a 10–30 ms delay mixed with the original, no feedback
  - `flanger` — flanger: a 0–5 ms delay with feedback, a comb of sweeping notches
  - `vibrato` — vibrato: the delayed signal alone, so the pitch wobbles
- `rate_hz` — LFO frequency, Hz: how fast the delay sweeps
- `depth_ms` — Sweep width, ms: the delay moves from base_ms to base_ms + depth_ms
- `base_ms` — Shortest delay, ms; it never gets shorter than one sample
- `feedback` — Share of the delayed signal fed back into the delay; flanger only
- `mix` — Share of the delayed signal in the output: 0 is dry only; vibrato always uses 1

### `phaser` — Phaser

- `stages` — Number of first-order allpass stages: every two give one notch in the response
- `rate_hz` — LFO frequency, Hz: how fast the notches move
- `min_hz` — Lower end of the stages' break-frequency sweep, Hz; below Nyquist
- `max_hz` — Upper end of the sweep, Hz; below Nyquist; the sweep is even in octaves
- `feedback` — Share of the chain output fed back to its input: sharper notches, more resonance
- `mix` — Share of the processed signal in the output: 0.5 gives the deepest notches

### `reverb` — Reverb

- `room_size` — Room size: larger means a longer tail (comb feedback 0.7…0.98)
- `damping` — High-frequency damping in the tail: higher sounds darker
- `wet` — Level of the reverberation; the input length is kept, so pad it to hear the tail
- `dry` — Level of the original signal in the output

### `wavefold` — Wavefolder

- `drive` — Gain before folding: higher gives more folds and overtones
- `shape` — How the signal folds
  - `triangle` — reflection off ±1: a triangular transfer curve
  - `sine` — y = sin(π/2·drive·x): smooth folds

### `am` — Amplitude modulation

- `carrier_hz` — Carrier frequency, Hz; above half the sample rate it aliases
- `depth` — Modulation index: where |depth·x| > 1 the envelope crosses zero (overmodulation)

### `fm` — Frequency modulation

- `carrier_hz` — Carrier frequency, Hz; above half the sample rate it aliases
- `deviation_hz` — Frequency deviation per unit of input, Hz: how far the frequency swings

### `gausspulse` — Gaussian pulse

- `fc` — Centre frequency, Hz
- `bw` — Fractional bandwidth at level bwr, a share of fc: smaller gives a longer pulse
- `bwr` — Level at which the bandwidth is measured, dB; negative
- `amplitude` — Peak amplitude; a negative value flips the polarity
- `rate` — Sample rate, Hz; frequencies above half of it alias
- `duration` — Signal length, s; the pulse sits in the middle

### `impulse_train` — Impulse train

- `period` — Period in samples, or seconds with an s suffix (0.01s)
- `offset` — Position of the first impulse: samples, or seconds with an s suffix
- `amplitude` — Impulse height; a negative value flips the polarity
- `rate` — Sample rate, Hz
- `duration` — Signal length, s

### `affine` — Scale and offset

- `scale` — Multiplier; a negative value flips the polarity
- `offset` — Constant added after the multiplication

### `math` — Function

- `op` — Function; outside its domain the block stops with an error instead of giving NaN
  - `log` — natural logarithm ln x; needs x > 0
  - `log10` — base-10 logarithm; needs x > 0; 20·log10 of the magnitude is the level in dB
  - `exp` — exponential eˣ; overflows above x = 709
  - `sqrt` — square root; needs x ≥ 0
  - `pow` — power xᵖ; for x < 0 p must be whole, for x = 0 not negative
  - `sign` — sign: −1, 0 or 1
  - `reciprocal` — reciprocal 1/x; needs x ≠ 0
- `p` — Exponent; pow only

### `binary` — Binary operation

- `op` — Operation; the shorter input is padded with zeros, as in add
  - `divide` — a / b; a zero in b, padding included, is an error
  - `min` — the smaller of a and b
  - `max` — the larger of a and b
  - `pow` — aᵇ; for a < 0 b must be whole, for a = 0 not negative
  - `hypot` — √(a² + b²) without overflow: the magnitude of two components

### `repeat` — Repeat

- `times` — How many copies the output holds; 1 leaves the signal as is

### `select_channel` — Select channel

- `index` — Channel number from zero: 0 is left, 1 is right

### `channel_delay` — Channel delay

- `delays` — Channel delays in samples, space- or comma-separated: 0 12; the last repeats, a negative one advances

### `stereo_width` — Stereo width

- `width` — Side-signal factor: 0 is mono, 1 changes nothing, 2 is twice as wide

### `balance` — Balance

- `position` — Position p: −1 keeps the left only (+3 dB), 0 changes nothing, 1 keeps the right only

### `sample_hold` — Sample and hold

- `hold` — Hold length: samples, or seconds with an s suffix; the effective rate drops to fs/hold

### `dc_block` — DC blocker

- `r` — Pole: closer to 1 lowers the cutoff, about (1−r)·fs/2π, and slows settling

### `lfilter` — Filter from coefficients b, a

- `b` — Numerator coefficients b0 b1 …, space- or comma-separated; divided by a0
- `a` — Denominator coefficients a0 a1 …; a0 must not be zero, a lone 1 gives an FIR filter

### `fir_taps` — FIR filter from taps

- `taps` — Impulse response h0 h1 …, space- or comma-separated
- `compensate` — Whether to remove the (N−1)/2-sample delay of an N-tap filter
  - `no` — output lags by (N−1)/2 samples, like a real causal filter
  - `yes` — output shifted back by (N−1)/2, time-aligned with the input

### `savgol` — Savitzky–Golay smoothing

- `window` — Window length in samples, odd: longer smooths more
- `polyorder` — Polynomial order, below the window: higher keeps peaks sharper but smooths less
- `deriv` — Derivative order: 0 smooths, 1 gives the slope per second, 2 the curvature
- `mode` — How the window is filled past the ends of the signal
  - `interp` — fit a polynomial to the first and last window instead of padding
  - `mirror` — reflect about the end samples without repeating them
  - `nearest` — repeat the end samples
  - `constant` — pad with zeros
  - `wrap` — continue periodically from the other end

### `hilbert` — Hilbert transform

- `output` — What to output; the magnitude is the envelope block, the phase rate is inst_freq
  - `imag` — the Hilbert transform itself: the signal shifted by 90°
  - `phase` — instantaneous phase in radians, wrapped to −π…π
  - `unwrapped` — instantaneous phase without 2π jumps, like numpy unwrap

### `remez` — Equiripple FIR (Remez)

- `numtaps` — Number of taps N: more gives a sharper transition but a (N−1)/2-sample delay
- `bands` — Band edges in Hz in pairs, from 0 up to nyquist, e.g. 0 1000 1500 nyquist
- `desired` — Wanted gain in each band, one value per pair of edges
- `weight` — Error weight per band, empty means equal; larger makes that band flatter
- `type` — Kind of filter to design
  - `bandpass` — ordinary filter: low-, high-, band-pass or band-stop by the bands
  - `differentiator` — gain grows with frequency: a derivative over the bands
  - `hilbert` — 90° phase shifter, an FIR Hilbert transformer
- `compensate` — Whether to remove the (N−1)/2-sample delay of an N-tap filter
  - `no` — output lags by (N−1)/2 samples, like a real causal filter
  - `yes` — output shifted back by (N−1)/2, time-aligned with the input

### `firls` — Least-squares FIR

- `numtaps` — Number of taps N, odd: more gives a sharper transition but a (N−1)/2-sample delay
- `bands` — Band edges in Hz in pairs, from 0 up to nyquist, e.g. 0 1000 1500 nyquist
- `desired` — Wanted gain at every band edge; the gain is linear in between
- `weight` — Error weight per band, empty means equal; larger fits that band closer
- `compensate` — Whether to remove the (N−1)/2-sample delay of an N-tap filter
  - `no` — output lags by (N−1)/2 samples, like a real causal filter
  - `yes` — output shifted back by (N−1)/2, time-aligned with the input

### `wpt` — Wavelet packets

- `wavelet` — Wavelet for every split; an orthogonal one keeps the energy and suits the best basis
- `levels` — Depth J of the full tree; the signal needs at least 2^J samples
- `basis` — Which leaves of the tree to output, in frequency order
  - `best` — Coifman–Wickerhauser best basis by Shannon entropy, one for all channels
  - `full` — all 2^J nodes of the deepest level: equal bands
  - `dwt` — aJ and dJ…d1: the same tiling as an ordinary DWT
- `ext` — Signal extension at the edges, as in pywt
  - `symmetric` — mirror repeating the edge: … x1 x0 | x0 x1 …
  - `periodization` — circular, non-expansive: ⌈N/2⌉ coefficients per level
  - `zero` — zeros beyond the edges
  - `constant` — repeats the edge sample
  - `smooth` — linear extrapolation from the two edge samples
  - `periodic` — periodic repetition, bands get longer
  - `reflect` — mirror without repeating the edge: … x2 x1 | x0 x1 x2 …
  - `antisymmetric` — mirror with a sign flip: … −x1 −x0 | x0 x1 …
  - `antireflect` — point reflection about the edge sample: x[−n] = 2x0 − x[n]

### `keep_largest` — Keep the largest

- `mode` — How amount sets K: a share of the coefficients or their number
  - `percent` — K is amount percent of the coefficients in the pool
  - `count` — K is amount itself, rounded to a whole number
- `amount` — Percentage or count of coefficients kept in each channel
- `keep_approx` — Whether the approximation is kept whole and left out of the selection
  - `yes` — approximation kept whole, K is chosen among the details
  - `no` — approximation competes with the details for the K places

### `coeff_quantize` — Quantize coefficients

- `mode` — How the step Δ is set
  - `bits` — per band: Δ = max|c| / (2^(bits−1) − 1)
  - `step` — one step Δ = step for every detail band
- `bits` — Bits per coefficient, sign included: fewer bits mean a coarser step
- `step` — Quantizer step Δ in coefficient units; values below Δ/2 become zero

### `shift` — Shift

- `samples` — Shift in samples: positive delays the signal, negative advances it
- `mode` — What fills the vacated samples
  - `zero` — zeros enter, samples pushed past the edge are lost
  - `circular` — circular shift: samples leaving one end come back at the other

### `biquad` — Biquad (RBJ)

- `type` — Filter type; the cookbook formula for b and a
  - `lowpass` — low-pass: −3 dB at f0 for q = 0.7071
  - `highpass` — high-pass: −3 dB at f0 for q = 0.7071
  - `bandpass` — band-pass with 0 dB at f0; q sets the width
  - `notch` — notch: zero gain at f0, q sets the width
  - `peaking` — peaking EQ: gain_db at f0, 0 dB far from it
  - `lowshelf` — low shelf: gain_db below f0
  - `highshelf` — high shelf: gain_db above f0
  - `allpass` — all-pass: flat magnitude, the phase turns by 360° around f0
- `f0` — Centre or corner frequency, Hz; must lie between 0 and Nyquist
- `q` — Quality factor: higher is narrower and more resonant
- `gain_db` — Gain at f0 or on the shelf, dB; for peaking, lowshelf and highshelf only

### `compressor` — Compressor

- `detector` — How the level is measured; channels are linked by one gain
  - `peak` — instantaneous peak |x| over all channels
  - `rms` — mean square over all channels, averaged over rms_ms
- `threshold_db` — Threshold, dBFS: the level above which compression starts
- `ratio` — Compression ratio: 4 means 4 dB over threshold become 1 dB
- `knee_db` — Knee width, dB: 0 is a hard knee, more makes the onset gradual
- `attack_ms` — Attack time constant, ms: how fast the gain drops when the level rises
- `release_ms` — Release time constant, ms: how fast the gain recovers when the level falls
- `makeup_db` — Makeup gain, dB, added after compression
- `rms_ms` — Averaging time of the RMS detector, ms; rms only

### `limiter` — Limiter

- `threshold_db` — Ceiling, dBFS: with attack 0 no sample ends above it
- `attack_ms` — Attack time constant, ms: above 0 short peaks slip past the ceiling
- `release_ms` — Release time constant, ms: how fast the gain recovers after a peak

### `gate` — Noise gate

- `threshold_db` — Threshold, dBFS: below it the gate closes
- `range_db` — Attenuation of the closed gate, dB: 80 is nearly silence
- `attack_ms` — Opening time constant, ms: how fast the gate opens on a loud sound
- `release_ms` — Closing time constant, ms: how fast the gate closes after it

### `echo` — Echo

- `delay_ms` — Time between repeats, ms
- `feedback` — Share of each repeat fed back: 0 gives one echo, near 1 a long tail
- `mix` — Wet share: 0 is the dry signal only, 1 the echoes only

### `comb` — Comb filter

- `kind` — Which signal is delayed
  - `feedback` — y[n] = x[n] + g·y[n−D]: resonant peaks, an IIR filter
  - `feedforward` — y[n] = x[n] + g·x[n−D]: evenly spaced notches, an FIR filter
- `delay` — Delay D in samples, or seconds with an s suffix (0.01s); at least one sample
- `gain` — Gain g of the delayed path; feedback needs |g| < 1

### `mix` — Mixer

- `gain_a_db` — Gain of input a, dB
- `gain_b_db` — Gain of input b, dB
- `mode` — How the inputs are combined; the shorter one is zero-padded
  - `sum` — sum of both inputs at every sample
  - `crossfade` — a until at, a linear fade over crossfade_ms, then b
- `at` — Start of the crossfade, s; crossfade only
- `crossfade_ms` — Crossfade length, ms; 0 switches at once; crossfade only

### `lpc` — Linear prediction (LPC)

- `order` — Predictor order p: how many past samples predict the next one

### `spectrum_peaks` — Spectrum peaks

- `count` — How many of the strongest peaks to report
- `segment` — Welch segment, samples, overlap 50 %: longer resolves closer peaks
- `prominence_db` — Minimum prominence, dB: how far a peak must rise above its surroundings

### `band_power` — Octave band power

- `bands` — Band width; centres are the IEC 61260 base-10 series around 1 kHz
  - `octave` — octave bands from 31.5 Hz to 16 kHz
  - `third` — third-octave bands from 25 Hz to 20 kHz
- `segment` — Welch segment, samples, overlap 50 %: longer resolves the low bands better

### `pitch` — Pitch (YIN)

- `fmin` — Lowest pitch to look for, Hz: lower needs a longer frame
- `fmax` — Highest pitch to look for, Hz
- `frame` — Frame length in samples; its first half is the comparison window
- `hop` — Step between frames in samples; the f0 output runs at rate/hop
- `threshold` — Threshold on the normalised difference: lower is stricter, more frames unvoiced

### `sst_band` — Synchrosqueezed band

- `lo` — Lower edge of the band, Hz
- `hi` — Upper edge of the band, Hz; at most half the sample rate
- `voices` — Voices per octave: how many CWT scales cover each doubling of frequency; more rebuilds more exactly and takes longer
- `mu` — Morlet parameter μ: larger gives each scale a narrower band and a longer reach in time

### `sst_ridge` — Synchrosqueezed ridge

- `lo` — Lower edge of the search band, Hz
- `hi` — Upper edge of the search band, Hz; at most half the sample rate
- `voices` — Voices per octave: how many CWT scales cover each doubling of frequency
- `mu` — Morlet parameter μ: larger gives a more exact frequency and a coarser time resolution

### `mfcc` — MFCC

- `n_mfcc` — How many coefficients to report, starting with c0, the overall level
- `n_mels` — Number of triangular mel filters from 0 Hz to Nyquist
- `frame` — Frame length in samples, Hann window
- `hop` — Step between frames in samples

### `spectral_features` — Spectral features

- `frame` — Frame length in samples, Hann window: longer is finer in frequency
- `hop` — Step between frames in samples
- `rolloff_pct` — Rolloff is the frequency below which this percentage of the magnitudes lies

### `histogram` — Histogram

- `bins` — Number of equal-width bins
- `range` — auto takes the signal's minimum and maximum, or give two numbers lo hi

### `coeff_stats` — Coefficient statistics

- `tol` — A coefficient counts as near zero below tol × the largest magnitude in the channel

### `time_stretch` — Time stretch

- `rate` — How many times faster: above 1 is shorter, below 1 longer; the pitch stays
- `method` — How to stretch
  - `vocoder` — phase vocoder as in librosa: STFT magnitudes are interpolated, phases accumulated; smooth on tones, smears transients
  - `locked` — vocoder with phase locking (Laroche–Dolson): bins around a peak take the peak's phase — less phasiness, a fuller sound
  - `wsola` — WSOLA in time: signal pieces shift within hop to join by shape; keeps transients, good for a single voice
- `n_fft` — STFT window length (the piece length for WSOLA), samples, even: longer is sharper in frequency, blurrier in time
- `hop` — Window step, samples, at most n_fft/2: smaller is smoother and slower; for WSOLA also the piece shift limit

### `pitch_shift` — Pitch shift

- `semitones` — Shift in semitones, fractions allowed: 12 is an octave up, −12 an octave down, 7 a fifth
- `method` — How to stretch
  - `vocoder` — phase vocoder as in librosa: STFT magnitudes are interpolated, phases accumulated; smooth on tones, smears transients
  - `locked` — vocoder with phase locking (Laroche–Dolson): bins around a peak take the peak's phase — less phasiness, a fuller sound
  - `wsola` — WSOLA in time: signal pieces shift within hop to join by shape; keeps transients, good for a single voice
- `n_fft` — STFT window length (the piece length for WSOLA), samples, even: longer is sharper in frequency, blurrier in time
- `hop` — Window step, samples, at most n_fft/2: smaller is smoother and slower; for WSOLA also the piece shift limit

### `speed` — Speed, like a record

- `rate` — How many times faster; the pitch moves with the speed: 2 is an octave up, 0.5 an octave down
